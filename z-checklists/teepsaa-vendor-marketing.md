# teepsaa — Vendor Marketing Playbook

The real-world plan for recruiting the first vendors: **as many Phnom Penh
clothing shops as possible**, on foot, founder-led. This is the cold-start
engine for the whole marketplace. No orders without vendors.

Owner-led by a foreign founder + a Khmer voice (girlfriend/partner) for
language, rapport, and support.

---

## Guiding strategy (the "why" behind everything below)

- **Go narrow and dense first.** Not "all of Cambodia, everything." One city
  (Phnom Penh), one category (clothing). A marketplace dense in one niche beats
  one thin everywhere.
- **The first 20–50 vendors come from hustle, not ads.** Recruit them by hand,
  in person, one shop at a time.
- **Commission is a vendor-acquisition weapon right now.** Keep it low to lower
  the barrier. Raise it later once vendors depend on your traffic.
- **Happy early vendors are your real sales engine.** Khmer-to-Khmer word of
  mouth beats any pitch — it routes around the "foreign founder" question
  entirely.

---

## The offer: grandfathered 5% "Founding Vendor" rate

- Current royalty: **5%** — low end of the market (Etsy ~6.5%, eBay ~10–13%,
  Amazon ~8–15%). Deliberately low to attract the first sellers.
- **Pitch it as a "Founding Vendor" rate, out loud.** "Join now and lock in 5%
  forever, even when we raise it for new sellers later."
- **Grandfather the early vendors.** When you raise the rate later, keep the
  first sellers at 5% as a permanent loyalty perk; apply the higher rate only to
  _new_ signups. This avoids the raise-and-churn trap that burns marketplaces.
- Plan the path to ~8–12% for later cohorts, once you deliver traffic vendors
  can't get elsewhere. Use per-category rates — higher-margin categories can
  bear more.
- **Know your number:** at 5% on a ~$15 order = ~$0.75/order. A ~$1,500/mo
  living wage ≈ ~67 orders/day. Track order volume against that target.

---

## Who to target FIRST (this matters more than founder nationality)

The easiest, best early vendors are the ones **already selling online**:

- [ ] Facebook Live / Facebook Marketplace clothing sellers
- [ ] Telegram / Instagram clothing shops
- [ ] Younger, digital-native stall owners (Toul Tom Poung / Russian Market,
      boutique streets, online-first brands)

Why them: they already grasp e-commerce (no need to teach the concept from
zero), they skew more English-capable, and they're your natural dense niche.

**Save the hard segment for later:** cash-only, walk-in-only traditional market
stalls are a harder e-commerce sell regardless of who pitches them. Don't
measure your odds against the hardest vendors first.

---

## The two-person field team

- **Founder (you): lead.** Vision, product knowledge, the app demo, motivation,
  showing up. Your drive is a moat a hired salesperson won't match.
- **Khmer voice (partner): language + rapport + support.** The trust bridge —
  handles the relationship conversation, objections, indirect "no," and
  ongoing vendor support in Khmer.
- Together you signal _both_ "serious/professional/real tech" (foreign founder)
  AND "I can trust and reach these people" (Khmer partner). That combination
  often reads as more credible than a lone founder either way.

### If the Khmer voice is your girlfriend/partner — ground rules

- [ ] Confirm she genuinely wants in (believes in it), not just pleasing you.
- [ ] Test her comfort with sales on a few shops before assuming it's her role.
- [ ] **Keep business ownership 100% in the founder's name.** Foreigners CAN own
      100% of a Cambodian company — you do NOT need a Khmer co-owner. Do not tie
      the company's legal control to the relationship.
- [ ] **Land is the only foreigner restriction** — never put land, or the
      company itself, in a partner's name to "solve" a problem that doesn't
      exist for businesses. Get local legal advice on structure independently.
- [ ] Define her role and upside in writing (salary / commission / stake) as it
      grows — prevents resentment killing both business and relationship.

---

## Trust-builders for a foreign-founded platform

The vendor's real questions are: _Will I get paid? Is this legit? Can I reach
someone in Khmer? Will you still be here in a year?_ Answer all four:

- [ ] Register a real local company (Cambodia allows 100% foreign ownership).
- [ ] Local phone number + Khmer support channel (Telegram).
- [ ] Learn functional Khmer sales phrases — even imperfect Khmer massively
      signals "committed to _us_" and kills the "transient foreigner" doubt.
- [ ] Show up in person, repeatedly. Presence over time converts "transient"
      into "committed."
- [ ] Collect testimonials/logos from the first happy vendors; show them to the
      next shop.
- [ ] Make the payout flow fast and visibly reliable — nothing builds trust like
      the first vendor getting paid on time and telling others.

---

## Field kit (build before hitting the streets)

- [ ] **Visual walkthrough guide** — picture-based, Khmer + English, showing:
      sign up → list a product → receive an order → dispatch → get paid. Works
      even if the vendor speaks no English.
- [ ] One-page flyer / business card with QR to sign-up + Telegram support.
- [ ] A live demo on your phone (a real seeded shop the vendor can see working).
- [ ] Short Khmer script/FAQ for the partner: the pitch, the 5% Founding Vendor
      offer, top objections + answers.
- [ ] Simple onboarding: help them create the account + list their first 1–3
      products **on the spot**, so they leave already live (don't rely on
      "sign up later" — they won't).

---

## The daily hustle loop

- [ ] Pick a target zone (a market, a boutique street) per day.
- [ ] Walk in, partner opens in Khmer, you show the live demo.
- [ ] Onboard on the spot — first products listed before you leave.
- [ ] Log every shop: name, contact, signed up? / follow-up needed.
- [ ] Follow up within a few days on the maybes.
- [ ] Ask every happy vendor for 1–2 referrals to other shops (this is the
      real growth channel).

---

## Metrics to track (know if it's working)

- [ ] Shops approached / week
- [ ] Sign-up conversion rate (approached → live vendor)
- [ ] Vendors who list ≥1 product (activated) vs. dead sign-ups
- [ ] Vendors who get their first _order_ (the real activation)
- [ ] Referrals generated per happy vendor
- [ ] Orders/day trending toward the ~67/day (at 5%) living-wage target

---

## Milestones

- [ ] First 10 vendors live (proof the pitch works)
- [ ] First real buyer order flowing to a vendor
- [ ] First vendor paid out on time (trust proof to show others)
- [ ] First referral-sourced vendor (word of mouth is working)
- [ ] 50 vendors live in clothing / Phnom Penh (dense niche established)
- [ ] Enough volume + leverage to introduce a higher rate for _new_ cohorts
      (early vendors stay grandfathered at 5%)
